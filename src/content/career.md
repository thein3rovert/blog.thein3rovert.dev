## Career

When I started my placement, I was still figuring things out. I knew I wanted to become a software engineer, but that was about the extent of it.

In the first month, all placement students went through a series of talks. They covered practical things like how to navigate the year, how to speak up, and how to think more broadly about career and life beyond day-to-day tasks. Most of them were useful in the moment and then faded. One of them didn’t.

It was a session by the CTO at the time called “Where Placement Takes You.” It’s still one of the strongest presentations I’ve sat through, and it quietly shifted how I think about my own direction. He spoke about his career goals and how he mapped them out across 5, 10, even 30 years. What stayed with me wasn’t just the scale of it, but the discipline behind it — the idea that you can treat your career as something you actively design, not just something you react to.

That idea landed with me more than I expected. I’m someone who thinks in plans and visuals. I need some sense of direction to feel grounded in what I’m doing. So I decided to try the same approach.

Shortly after that talk, I started building my own career map. I wrote down the skills I had when I started placement, the ones I wanted to build, and the kinds of people I should be learning from. I mapped out stakeholders in a way that made sense to me, and I kept refining it over time. It’s not something I completed once and left, it’s something I return to whenever I’m making decisions that feel important. It helps me step back and check whether I’m still moving in the direction I originally set.

That presentation ended up shaping more than I expected. It changed how I think about planning, clarity, and ownership of my path.

Because of that, I’ve also tried to be more open about my own career goals and how they evolve. That’s part of why I’m putting this down here. Not because the path is fixed or figured out, but because writing it out makes it more intentional. If it helps someone else think a bit differently about their own direction, that’s a bonus. Either way, it keeps me honest about where I said I wanted to go.
