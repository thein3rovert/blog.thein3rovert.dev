## How One Presentation Changed the Way I Think About My Career

When I started my placement, I was still figuring things out. I knew I wanted to become a software engineer, but that was about the extent of it.

During my first month, all the placement students attended a series of talks. They covered practical things like navigating the placement year, speaking up, and thinking more broadly about our careers and lives beyond our day-to-day responsibilities.

Most of the talks were useful in the moment, and some of the lessons gradually became part of my everyday habits at work. Things I had to consciously think about at first eventually became second nature, and I am genuinely glad I had the opportunity to learn them. However, one of the presentations never faded into the background.

It was a session by the CTO at the time titled _“Where Placement Takes You.”_ It remains one of the strongest presentations I have attended, and it quietly changed how I think about my own direction.

He spoke about his career goals and how he mapped them out across five, ten, and even thirty years. What stayed with me was not just the scale of his ambitions, but the discipline behind them: the idea that you can treat your career as something you actively design, rather than something you simply react to as opportunities come along.

That idea resonated with me more than I expected. I am someone who thinks in plans and visuals. Having a sense of direction helps me feel grounded in what I am doing and gives me something to work towards. So, I decided to try the same approach.

Shortly after that talk, I started building my own career map. I documented the skills I had when I started my placement, the ones I wanted to develop, and the kinds of people I could learn from along the way. I also mapped out key stakeholders and relationships that could support my professional growth.

Since then, I have continued refining it as I gain experience and discover new interests. It is not something I completed once and left untouched; it is something I return to whenever I am making important decisions. It helps me step back, look at the bigger picture, and check whether I am still moving in the direction I originally set for myself.

That presentation ended up shaping more than I expected. It changed how I think about planning, having clarity, and taking ownership of my career path.

Because of that, I have also tried to be more open about my career goals and how they evolve over time. That is part of why I am sharing this here. Not because I have everything figured out or because my path is fixed, but because documenting it makes me more intentional about the decisions I make.

If sharing my experience helps someone else think a little differently about their own direction, that is a bonus. Either way, it gives me a way to hold myself accountable, reflect on how far I have come, and remember where I said I wanted to go.
